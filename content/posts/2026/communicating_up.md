My mentor had just come out of a two day strategic planning session with her board. The CEO was asking for votes, but he left empty handed. The board overwhelmingly responded that they couldn't approve his requests - they wanted to help, but they couldn't understand what he was asking for. What a waste of time. What a wasted opportunity.

No matter if you're a software engineer or a CEO, you'll be more successful if you speak in terms that your leadership will understand. It's [in your own best interest]({{< relref "dont-use-your-words" >}}) to make it easy for them. Simplifying the story is not pandering. Your area of responsibility, your corner of the business, is only one of many concerns they're juggling. They're not experts, and you shouldn't expect them to be. It's tempting to show how hard you worked. Tempting to walk them through your process and enumerate all of the options you considered. Don't do that. Give them the bottom line. Don't give them details unless they ask. If they don't understand a request, the answer will always be "No."

I said as much to my mentor. This executive should have realized that the people above him were generalists. He needed to put himself in their shoes. He needed to understand what mattered to them. He should have ruthlessly cut everything else.

She agreed. In fact, she planned to use my wording when she followed up with the CEO.
